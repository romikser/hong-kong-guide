Shek Kip Mei

An unusually coherent pairing of housing history and present-day making.

How this place became itself

The December 1953 fire and the subsequent resettlement blocks are fundamental to understanding Hong Kong’s public housing. Mei Ho House and the former factory estate now occupied by JCCAC give two complementary material records of housing and work.

It is a residential district with cultural reuse embedded within it, not an open-air museum. The two institutions help explain how state planning, domestic life and small industry shaped the city.

With a camera: Public approaches between Mei Ho House and JCCAC; keep estate access, private corridors and residents’ privacy distinct from museum access.

Heritage of Mei Ho House

anchor

One of the clearest ways to understand the vertical city: resettlement, domestic life and the public-housing response following the 1953 Shek Kip Mei fire. Household-scale exhibits make a better complement to street photography than another skyline deck.
